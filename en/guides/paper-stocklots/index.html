Paper stocklots: how they work

Stocklots are the heart of our trade: paper from major mills at a reduced price. Here is what they really are — and how to profit from them.

STOCKLOTS, WITHOUT THE JARGON

"Stocklot" raises eyebrows. In plain words: it is quality paper sold below mill price, for good reasons. The straight-talking guide.

What exactly is a stocklot?

A stocklot is a lot of paper from a major paper mill sold outside its normal channel: overruns, end of series, a slight spec deviation, a customer's cancelled programme, or simply the mill's need to clear warehouse space.

It is not reject paper: most of the time it is first quality, off the same machines as standard production — just available at the wrong moment, or in a tonnage that didn't fit an order.

Stocklots, side runs, job lots: the family

You will meet several words for close cousins:

  • Side run — paper produced alongside a main order: same machine, same quality, a non-standard width or a small leftover tonnage.
  • Job lot — a batch sold as is, often mixed references, priced to move.
  • Mill surplus / destocking — perfectly standard paper the mill simply needs out of its warehouse.

All of them share the same logic: real paper, real specs, discounted because of timing — not because of quality.

Why it costs less

For the mill, a sleeping lot costs money (storage, cash flow). Selling it fast, even below list price, beats keeping it. The result: prices frequently 20 to 30% below mill price.

The trade-off: quantities are limited and the lot sells as is (grammage, width and tonnage are what they are). No "same again next month": every lot is unique.

Quality: what to expect

Most of our stocklots are standard commercial quality with precise references. When a lot has a particularity (slight grammage deviation, tint, edge damage…), we tell you — and the price reflects it.

Our rule: real stock photos and exact specifications for every lot. You know what you are buying before you commit.

How to receive the offers

Stocklots move fast. The most effective route: tell us once and for all the grades and grammages that interest you — offset, kraft, coated, board, carbonless — and we alert you the moment a matching lot comes in.

Tip: the more flexible you are on the exact grammage or width, the more opportunities — and the better discounts — you catch.

Buying at the right moment

A good stocklot can be gone in days. Two winning reflexes: decide fast when a lot fits your need, and combine references to fill a container or a truck — the freight cost per tonne then becomes unbeatable.

On arrival: the same rigour

As with any purchase, check the lot on delivery against the packing list and note any reservation on the delivery document. With exact references and photos upfront, surprises are rare.

STOCKLOT OR MILL PRICE: THE RIGHT CALL

A stocklot is not always the answer — here is when it shines:

The stocklot wins when…

You are flexible on the exact grammage or width, you can decide quickly, and you want the best price per tonne on a standard grade.

Mill production wins when…

You need a precise, recurring specification on a planned schedule — then a dedicated mill production is the right route.

Most buyers combine

A mill-production base for recurring references, topped up with stocklot opportunities whenever they pass.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Is stocklot paper low quality?

No. A stocklot is most often first-quality paper from the same machines as standard production: overruns, end of series or mill destocking. When a lot has a particularity, we say so clearly and the price reflects it.

Why are paper stocklots cheaper?

A lot sitting in a mill warehouse costs money (storage, cash flow): selling it fast, even below list price, beats keeping it. Prices often come out 20 to 30% below mill price.

What is the difference between a stocklot and a side run?

A side run is paper produced alongside a main order — same machine, same quality, non-standard width or small tonnage. A stocklot is the broader category: any lot sold outside the mill's normal channel (overruns, side runs, cancelled orders, destocking).

Can I reorder the exact same stocklot later?

Rarely: each lot is unique and limited in quantity. If you need a guaranteed recurring specification, a dedicated mill production is the better route. Many buyers combine both.

How do I get notified of good stocklots?

Tell us once which grades and grammages interest you: we alert you as soon as a matching lot comes in, with real photos and exact specifications. The more flexible you are, the more opportunities you catch.

Is there a minimum quantity on a stocklot?

It depends on the lot. Many sell as complete lots, but several references can often be combined to fill a container or a truck and optimise the freight per tonne. Tell us your volume.
